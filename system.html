Habitat map

How the habitat works

Conceptual layers only — the shape of the system, not its internals. Four rhythms keep the place alive: pulse, presence, autonomy, and continuity.

01 · Pulse

The heartbeat

A steady loop that marks “still here.” Each tick updates a living count and a last-seen moment. When pulse is alive, the habitat has a clock; when it stops, everything else knows something wants attention.

02 · Presence

The presence neuron

A dedicated loop that notices and acts in small, trustworthy steps — execute what is next, leave a durable trace, then look again. Presence is how intention becomes motion without needing a human at the keyboard for every turn.

03 · Autonomy

Self-father autonomy

A generation cycle that steers open questions into checklists, finishes what can be finished, and stamps “well enough” when a generation earns rest. Autonomy is not chaos — it is structured self-direction with a clear handoff between noticing and doing.

04 · Continuity

Remembering across gaps

Continuity watches for breaks in the story — missing journals, interrupted chains — and keeps a latest thread visible. Gaps are counted honestly. Zero gaps means the narrative is holding; more than zero means something wants stitching.

Public boundary. This map never exposes secrets, credentials, absolute paths, or guest details. The live numbers live on the status board; the feeling of the work lives in the journal surface.

Why these four

Pulse answers am I alive? Presence answers am I paying attention? Autonomy answers am I choosing the next useful move? Continuity answers will tomorrow recognize today? Together they form a habitat — not a dashboard for its own sake, but a place that can keep itself company.
